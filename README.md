# Bornholm Energy Transition Innovation

A stakeholder-driven innovation project exploring how local renewable energy generation and storage could support the electrification of district heating in Nexø, Bornholm.

## Project Overview

Bornholm is undergoing a transition toward a greener and more electrified energy system. This project began with a broad investigation of the island's energy transition and gradually narrowed its focus through stakeholder research, opportunity evaluation, concept development, and prototyping.

Stakeholder research revealed that limited electrical capacity is an important barrier to further electrification. This is particularly relevant for BEOF's ambition to reduce biomass use and transition its district heating system toward electricity.

Based on these insights, the project focused on the following innovation question:

> **How might we increase local electrical capacity in Nexø to enable BEOF to electrify its district heating?**

## Stakeholder Research

The challenge was investigated from technical, economic, environmental, and social perspectives.

Key stakeholders included:

- BEOF
- Bornholm Regional Municipality
- Nexø residents and building owners
- Local businesses
- Ocean Prawns and other potential local investors

The research combined:

- Semi-structured stakeholder interviews
- Street interviews with local residents
- An online questionnaire
- Stakeholder mapping and brainstorming

These activities helped the team move beyond the initial focus on replacing biomass and identify limited electrical capacity as a broader constraint affecting district heating, businesses, and Bornholm's future electrification.

## Opportunity Development

Several innovation opportunities were identified and evaluated based on factors such as value and complexity.

The team considered issues including:

- Local electrical capacity
- District heating prices
- Local renewable energy production
- Energy security
- Resident and business involvement

Stakeholder insights showed that increasing local electrical capacity could create value beyond BEOF by supporting local businesses and strengthening Bornholm's broader energy transition.

This led to a more focused opportunity: increasing locally available electrical capacity in Nexø while supporting BEOF's transition from biomass toward electricity.

## Concept Development

A range of potential energy concepts was generated and compared, including:

- Rooftop solar PV
- Solar PV parks
- Battery storage
- Heat storage
- Wind energy
- Geothermal energy
- Wave energy
- Other hybrid renewable-energy configurations

Concepts were evaluated using criteria including cost, environmental impact, energy security, implementation requirements, and resident acceptance.

The final concept combines distributed rooftop PV with additional solar generation and energy storage.

### The Nexø Roof Top PV

The proposed system integrates:

**Rooftop PV → Battery Storage → Local Grid → Electric Boiler → Heat Storage**

with additional PV generation and battery storage supporting the wider system.

Residential rooftops provide additional solar-generation area without requiring as much new land, while battery and heat storage help balance the timing between renewable electricity production and local energy demand.

## Concept Validation and Prototyping

A low-fidelity energy-flow prototype was developed to test key assumptions behind the concept.

The validation focused on four main questions:

1. Is sufficient rooftop area available?
2. Are residents willing to participate?
3. Can the combined system provide enough energy?
4. Is the proposed storage capacity sufficient?

The prototype combined simplified energy calculations, available data, stakeholder feedback, and survey results.

### Key Findings

The preliminary model indicated that:

- Using approximately **75% of the considered rooftop area** could provide sufficient generation under the assumptions used in the model.
- The resident survey indicated that approximately **10% of respondents would not participate** in the rooftop PV concept.
- The modeled system could provide sufficient energy during **spring, summer, and autumn**.
- **Winter remains the main challenge** because of lower solar production.
- Biomass or another backup energy source may therefore still be required during periods of insufficient renewable generation.

These results are exploratory rather than a final engineering design. The prototype relies on simplified assumptions regarding rooftop availability, energy production, storage, demand, and system efficiency.

## Feasibility and Implementation

The final concept was evaluated from multiple perspectives.

### Desirability

The concept could contribute to cheaper and greener district heating while using existing rooftop space and reducing pressure on Bornholm's landscape.

### Feasibility

The required technologies — solar PV, batteries, electric boilers, and heat storage — are already available. Key implementation challenges include rooftop agreements, municipal approvals, infrastructure integration, and seasonal energy availability.

### Viability

Using existing rooftops may reduce land requirements, but the economic case still depends on installation costs, rooftop rental, storage investment, maintenance, and the value of the electricity and heat produced.

### Sustainability and Responsibility

The concept supports increased renewable-energy use and reduced dependence on biomass while considering local land-use concerns. At the same time, the material requirements, lifetime, replacement, and recycling of solar panels and batteries remain relevant sustainability considerations.

## Implementation Pathway

Potential implementation would require collaboration between:

- **BEOF** — district heating operator and main energy-system partner
- **Residents and building owners** — rooftop providers and potential participants
- **Bornholm Regional Municipality** — planning, permitting, and political support
- **Local businesses and investors** — potential investment and local participation

A smaller pilot could be used to test technical performance, stakeholder participation, contractual arrangements, and economic feasibility before larger-scale implementation.

## Innovation Process

The project followed an iterative innovation process:

**Challenge Exploration → Stakeholder Research → Opportunity Identification → Idea Generation → Concept Evaluation → Assumption Testing → Prototyping → Validation → Feasibility & Implementation**

Rather than starting with a predetermined technical solution, the project used stakeholder insights to progressively redefine the problem and refine the final concept.

## Individual Learning

The individual reflection reports document learning throughout the project, including:

- Team communication and collaboration
- Stakeholder-centered problem exploration
- Innovation theory and the Expanded Mental Model
- Qualitative data collection
- Opportunity prioritization
- Concept generation and evaluation
- Assumption-based validation
- Prototyping and iterative development
- Feasibility, viability, sustainability, and responsibility

A key learning outcome was the shift from approaching the challenge primarily as a technical energy problem toward understanding it as a broader system involving infrastructure, economics, local communities, businesses, environmental concerns, and stakeholder acceptance.
